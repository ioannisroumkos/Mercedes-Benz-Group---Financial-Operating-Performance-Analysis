# Mercedes-Benz Group — Financial & Operating Performance Analysis (2021–2025)

A five-year financial and operating performance analysis of Mercedes-Benz Group AG covering 2021–2025, with a 2026 operating outlook and valuation framework based on information publicly available through **7 October 2026**.

## Research question

**How has Mercedes-Benz's financial and operating performance evolved from 2021 to 2025, what factors have driven the changes, and how effectively has the company converted its operating performance into cash and shareholder returns?**

## Investment-research thesis

Mercedes-Benz is undergoing an earnings reset, with the new product cycle central to the path toward sustainable margins and returns.

## Scope of the analysis

The report examines:

- Group revenue, EBIT and profitability
- Mercedes-Benz Cars, Vans and Mobility
- Unit volumes, pricing, product mix and revenue quality
- China exposure and the BBAC earnings channel
- Electrification, BEV adoption and the original 2025 xEV ambition
- Product and technology investment intensity
- Industrial ROIC and its operating drivers
- Industrial free cash flow, liquidity and shareholder returns
- Cash conversion and working capital
- BMW peer comparison
- 2026 operating outlook and valuation framework

The analysis contains **21 exhibits**. Company-reported financial KPIs are kept separate from project-derived analytical calculations.

## Key findings

- Mercedes-Benz experienced a significant earnings reset between 2022 and 2025, with Group adjusted EBIT falling from €20.7bn to €8.2bn.
- Cars absorbed most of the deterioration, while Vans remained comparatively resilient.
- China was the principal geographic pressure point, accounting for approximately 72% of the net global Cars volume decline in 2025.
- Industrial ROIC fell from 32.6% in 2022 to 11.2% in 2025, driven primarily by the decline in NOPAT margin.
- Industrial free cash flow remained positive and net industrial liquidity reached €32.2bn at year-end 2025.
- Q3 2026 provides early evidence of new-product-cycle traction: Group BEVs rose 52% year on year, while Cars BEVs rose 61%, although China Cars sales fell 31% in the quarter.

## Methodology

The model combines company-reported historical financial information with clearly identified analytical calculations. Reported Group and segment KPIs are not treated as interchangeable with project-derived metrics.

The analytical framework focuses on the post-Daimler Truck separation period and keeps Mercedes-Benz Mobility separate from the industrial operating business when measuring industrial returns and liquidity.
